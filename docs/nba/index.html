    How the multis are picked
    Model
    This season's games (recent games count extra; the weekly tune sets how much) blended with last season (last season counts as 8 games' worth, less if he played under 40 games), shifted towards games played in tonight's situation (a teammate in or out), adjusted for the opponent's defence against his position and last season's head-to-head (shrunk by how much of the opponent's roster is still the same, and by sample size), adjusted for home or away (from his own split, shrunk for small samples), tonight's expected pace (the game total against the slate average) and tonight's line against his team's usual result (his averages already reflect how good his team is, so only a line tougher or easier than usual moves the prediction), scaled by expected minutes (cut on the second night of a back-to-back, and for one-sided games by whatever the weekly tune finds: past seasons show starters' minutes barely drop on average, so that cut is small), and blended 60/40 with the bookies' over/under line (margin removed) where one is available. The weekly tune fits the line and blowout settings on past seasons, standing in for old betting lines with each team's results up to that day, and keeps them only if they predict the newer seasons better than no adjustment.
    Chance all land
    The legs' chances multiplied, adjusted for same-game links: two rebound legs from one game work against each other, two points legs from one team share the shots, a team to win works against legs from the team it's playing, other same-game legs move a little together with pace.
    Value, marginal, skip
    Each multi is rated from the price one app pays for all its legs against the price it needs. Value: the price beats break-even by 5% or more. Skip: it falls 3% or more short. Marginal: in between, too close to call given the chances are estimates. The top of the page lists the value multis, or says to skip the day when there are none.
    Needs
    1 ÷ the chance all legs land. If the app's price for the whole multi is at or above it, the model says it's worth taking.
    Pays
    Each multi is priced as a whole at TAB, Neds and Bet365, and shows the app whose prices for every leg multiply to the most. When no one app prices every leg yet, an estimate is shown instead (fair price less a typical margin); check it in the app.
    Price in the app
    Type the price on offer and the slip suggests a share of your betting budget: a quarter of the Kelly amount ((chance × price − 1) ÷ (price − 1)), at most 3%, and nothing when the price is under break-even. Prices you type stay on your phone only.
    Same-game prices
    Apps price legs from one game together, so a same-game multi rarely pays the legs multiplied. When you finish typing an app's price into a slip, it's compared with the legs multiplied and the difference is kept on this phone (never uploaded). The average, starting from a guess of 12% under for three or more legs in a game, sets the price the slips and the promo builder tell you to expect.
    Promo builder
    Searches every rated leg from games yet to start for the multi that meets the offer's terms and is worth the most with the bonus counted: stake back as a bonus bet is valued at the share you set (70% by default), and the chance exactly one leg misses is worked out from the legs' chances.
    Team to win
    A team's chance to win blends the bookies' head-to-head prices (margin removed, 70%) with the betting line (30%). Teams 60%+ likely are rated in every game and go into any multi when TAB, Neds or Bet365 pays $1.20 or more. A less likely team is rated only when one of those apps pays more than its chance is worth.
    Which legs a multi can use
    The 16 likeliest legs, plus up to 8 less likely ones that are clearly better value: chance × app price at least 3c per $1 above the likeliest legs' typical value. Only legs with a real app price can get in this way, because a leg that merely looks like value is often one the model has overrated. Assists start at 2+ and threes at 1+.
    Late checks
    15–75 minutes before each game, its injury news is checked again. The morning's multis stay as they were (you may have bet them), but a leg whose player is ruled out is marked and its multis get a warning with a suggested swap. In the last 35 minutes before a tip, once the starting line-ups are posted, a usual starter who isn't in the starting five is marked "not starting". A starter who came off the bench in his last two games (or a bench player who started them) is predicted on those games' minutes.
    Rules
    No leg priced under $1.20 (where no price is known, the line is stepped up until the model is about 83% or lower). Never two rebound legs from the same game. At most two legs from one game in a 2-, 3- or 4-leg multi and three in a bigger one, so one bad game can't sink it. Never a team to win with a leg from the team it's playing. Players listed out or doubtful are left out.
    Prices
    Only TAB, Neds and Bet365. Each morning every game is priced at Neds (from OddsPapi), and the games the multis use most are also priced at TAB and Bet365 (from The Odds API, within its free daily credits).
    Overrides
    Edit overrides/nba.txt in the repo to skip a player, set minutes, mark someone out or enter odds. The next run uses it.
    Caveat
    Early-season samples are small and bookies price props well. This is analysis, not a guarantee.
